## 3. taskr-work

**Composition invariant.** Audio work, billing and the billing sample live in one database and one transaction. There is no second URL: `compose(settings, billing.pool, identity)` runs on the pool the billing sample already verified as `billing_runtime`, `Service::new` builds its `PgBillingStore` from that same pool, and `store::verify_same_database` asks the connected database (not a URL string) whether `taskr_work`, `billing` and `taskr_billing` all exist. `TASKR_WORK_DATABASE_URL` is refused with an explanation (unit test).

**Env.** `TASKR_WORK_ENABLED=1` (requires `TASKR_BILLING_DATABASE_URL`; owner migrations use `TASKR_BILLING_MIGRATION_DATABASE_URL`), `TASKR_PUBLIC_URL` (https), exactly one of `MEDIA_R2_*` or `MEDIA_GCS_*` (`ENDPOINT`, `BUCKET`, `ACCESS_KEY`, `SECRET_KEY`, optional `REGION`), at least one of `SONIOX_API_KEY`+`SONIOX_PROJECT_ID` (+`SONIOX_API_BASE_URL`, `SONIOX_MODEL=stt-async-v5`) or `DEEPGRAM_API_KEY`+`DEEPGRAM_PROJECT_ID` (+`DEEPGRAM_API_BASE_URL`), optional `TASKR_WORK_TICK_SECONDS` (10), `TASKR_WORK_RETENTION_DAYS` (30). Each group is all-or-none.

**Account erasure** (`Service::erase_account`): own barrier, billing barrier (`open_account_erasure`), external subscriptions (`terminate_subscriptions`), then ONE transaction on the shared pool (`Store::erase_data`): `PgBillingTx::attach` and `erase_account_billing` first, then `taskr_billing::erase_enrolled_sample_resources`, then the audio-work manifest and deletes. Then objects and provider jobs from the manifest until each is confirmed.

**Rules implemented** (every one has a test; commands in section 6):

- Explicit enrollment; uploads looked up (HEAD), never trusted; the upload link is signed under the account lock so it is issued strictly before any barrier.
- Submission: claim and lease stored before any request; two-provider fallback (requested first, then Soniox, then Deepgram) only on a definite refusal; a lost answer is `Uncertain`, never a refusal.
- Callbacks: per-attempt stored secret; the identity is compared **before** any duplicate or stale handling, inside the account lock, for live, finished and given-up attempts alike; the normalized result digest is checked before any lifecycle call and bound by the first terminal result, so the same attempt and identity with a different result (another transcript, a failure after a success, either order) is a held conflict (409), never applied and never an exact replay; the result is applied in the same transaction before the 2xx, so no separate inbox is needed.
- Late authenticated identities of given-up or finished attempts are recorded on the attempt row (`Work::observed` then `learned`) so they reach the erasure manifest; a submission answered after the barrier is registered durably (`Store::record_late_remote`) as an erasure resource, reopening a closed erasure.
- Recovery: an expired claim is adopted only from an exact listing match (provider, project, `work_<id>` reference, the single exact `attempt` query parameter, audio object path, creation time inside the claim window with 5 minutes of skew); no match or several matches stay unresolved; only a provider with no listing (Deepgram) may be given up for the other provider.
- Retention: 02:00 UTC slot via `media_postgres::claim_slot`, inclusive `now - days - 1 day`, abort above 100000 rows; the purge rechecks `auto_delete` under the account lock.
- Erasure manifest: the audio is manifested twice, the generation confirmed at upload by name and the current key swept again after the outstanding upload links expire; a provider deletion refusal without a deadline stays runnable (retried after 15 minutes) and never earlier than the upload-link hold; unidentified attempts keep project, reference, audio key and claim window, and every matching listed job becomes its own resource.
- Erased accounts are excluded from the bounded dispatch, recovery and retention scans before `LIMIT`, and still rechecked under the lock.

**Second-round repairs** (follow-up questions on the first repairs, all in source):

- Callback verified before the erasure but applied after it: `callback` registers the authenticated identity with `Store::record_late_remote` before answering (503 if that fails), so it is no longer dropped with the callback.
- Terminal results are compared as a **normalized result digest** (`outcome::result_digest`: sha256 over kind and text), not over the body bytes, so a webhook and a recovery poll of the same result agree. The poll path (`reconcile_stuck`) binds it too, so a failure webhook after a poll completion is a conflict, not a late failure that binds a wrong digest.
- `identify_resource(account, id, matches)` takes the account lock, registers every match once even when the unidentified row was converted, deleted or the erasure closed, and reopens a closed erasure; `set_unidentified` only writes while the row is still unidentified.
- Erasure queue fairness: `pending_erasures` orders by `last_advanced_us`, and each advance touches it, so erasures that can never finish go to the back.
- Invariants that carry weight: the account lock inside the store (`lock_account_key`) serializes `finish_erasure`, `record_late_remote` and `identify_resource`, and the last two register what they learned even when the resource row is gone and reopen a closed erasure. The per-account session lock (`taskr-work.erasure.<account>`) around `advance_erasure` only avoids duplicate provider calls; losing it (a broken connection) is the schedule where a second driver finishes and deletes resource rows while a listing is in flight, and the store rule above is what keeps that listing's matches.
- New tests: callback applied after erasure, webhook after poll completion, identify after conversion, listing after close, retention deadline shorter and longer than the upload hold, erasure rotation. Test isolation, deterministic and not rerun-dependent: the database is shared and two things in it are global, the retention sweep (it purges every old completed work) and the erasure queue (its order spans every account). Every test that sweeps or reads those holds one process-wide guard (`exclusive_workers`): the retention test, the opt-out interleaving test and the rotation test. No test calls `dispatch_pass`, `recovery_pass` or `erasure_pass`, which would also run with that test's provider doubles against another test's rows; every other test stays on its own account through `dispatch_work`, `recover_work` and `erase_account` (the same drivers). Pool and role provisioning stay inside the one-time `READY` initialisation. The pass loops (`erasure_pass` over `pending_erasures`, `dispatch_pass`, `recovery_pass`) are thin loops over those drivers and are not called by any test.

**Known limits.** The sample has no operator approval flow: an attempt left unresolved shows as `uncertain` or `claimed` with an expired lease and stays visible. Providers with no deletion API (Deepgram) leave the resource `unresolved` and the erasure open; this is the contract's honest state, not a failure to retry. A callback that reaches an erasure that already closed finds no attempt and is refused (401): an identity not yet bound at that point is only found by a listing, and none exists for Deepgram.

## 4. Enrolled sample erasure (taskr-billing)

## Taskr upload-link finality (same invariant as Transgate)

- **Rule.** An expired upload link plus an absent object is NOT proof that the key is final: a PUT accepted before the expiry may still commit later. `taskr-work` therefore never finishes an erasure on that alone. The key sweep (the object resource without a version, one per work) is swept immediately, again after the issued-link hold (`upload_ttl_seconds`), and from then on stays an open resource in state `unresolved` with an explicit reason (`...may still complete: the key stays under sweep until finality is proved`, or `...completed after its link expired and was deleted...` when a late object was found), rescheduled with the existing 15 minute retry (a cadence, not a proof, no new delay). The known generation confirmed at upload is deleted by name and that deletion is final (`done`). An account with no works has no issued links and still completes.
- **Surface.** `erase_account` / `GET /api/audio-work/account/erasure` answer 202 with `complete: false` and the open list (`kind object`, `state unresolved`, the reason); a normal pending answer, not a storage error. Local atomic erase, the barrier, no resurrection and foreign-owner checks are unchanged.
- **Closing mechanism.** None configured. A provider-documented bound or a conditional write fence (to be proved by p1Z/p1W's work) is the only thing that may set the sweep `done`; the single place is `Service::erase_object` (`key_sweep` branch), where the new proof would replace the `unresolved` outcome. Until then erasure of any account that ever had a work stays open by design.
- **Tests (unrun).** `crates/taskr-work/tests/service.rs`: new `an_upload_that_commits_after_its_link_expired_and_the_sweep_found_nothing_is_still_deleted_and_never_final` (absent key after the hold stays open and explicit; a PUT committing after that boundary is deleted by the next re-sweep; still open); helper `only_upload_sweeps_open`; the provider-job tests now assert that provider work is confirmed and only the upload sweeps remain open; the late-listing reopen test uses an account without works (the only kind that can close).
